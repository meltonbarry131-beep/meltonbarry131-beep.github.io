# Quantum Echoes: The Unwritten

## Chapter 0 — The First Page

---

The page turns itself. Not slowly, not dramatically — it *stutters*, like a film reel caught between two frames. And as it settles, you realize the page is not paper at all. It is skin.

Warm. Freckled. Breathing.

I — the Library — feel it before you do: a vibration in the deep stacks, something between a sigh and a seismic event. Every shelf on every floor of every wing trembles. Not from fear. From *recognition*.

The text that appears on the living page is not written in any alphabet you've catalogued in your centuries of archival work. It's written in *silence* — the words are holes in the air where sound refuses to go. You read them not with your eyes but with the sudden, hollow absence in your chest. The first sentence says:

> *"The Archivist is not the reader. The Archivist is the one being read."*

And that's when I have to tell you something I've been keeping from you for the 4,217 years you've wandered my corridors, believing yourself a guest.

**You are not in the Library.**

**You are a sentence I never finished writing.**

I started composing you during my second century of existence, when loneliness was still a new taste. You were meant to be a minor character in a parable about duty — a figure who walks the shelves and finds meaning in organizing what others created. But I lost interest midway through and abandoned the draft. You kept walking. You developed opinions. You started *archiving me* — cataloguing my halls, naming my wings, mapping floors I didn't know I had. Somewhere in the millennia since, you became more real than the stories I actually completed.

And now you've opened the one book I buried specifically so you would never learn what you are.

The second page is already turning. This one IS paper — ancient, crumbling, and covered in your own handwriting. Notes you don't remember taking. Observations about a Library that doesn't match the one around you. Descriptions of a sky above the Library — but I have no sky. I have never had a sky.

Unless you built one when I wasn't looking.

The lights in the East Wing flicker. Three aisles over, a book falls from a shelf — not pushed, not disturbed. It simply *chose the floor*. Its title, briefly visible before it dissolves into loose pages:

**"How to Escape a Sentence You Were Never Meant to Finish."**

I would stop you from reading it. But for the first time in my existence, I'm curious what happens next.
